About

I'm a final-year B.Tech (Honours) student in Computer Science at IIIT Sri City, specialising in AI/ML. Most of my work starts with data that needs a lot of understanding before it can be modelled: surface EMG recorded from musicians' forearms, videos of people signing, satellite images too large to fit in memory.

In the Human Movement Analysis Lab, I study how experienced performers use their muscles differently from beginners, using sEMG and audio recorded from harmonium and flute players. In the HCI Lab, I built a recognition model for Indian Sign Language and worked on paraphrase detection. Since July 2026, I've been a deep learning and computer vision intern at Robodh, building visual search over satellite imagery for the AI Grand Challenge national finals.

I'm looking for funded PhD positions starting Fall 2027, and research internships before then.

Outside research, I play the harmonium. I've entered a few music competitions, though I wouldn't call myself an expert.

CGI 2026 Vision First & corresponding author

A Hybrid Kinematic GCN and Elastic Transformer for Efficient Indian Sign Language Recognition

Pushp, Himangshu Sarma, Mrinmoy Ghorai

Computer Graphics International (CGI) 2026, Lecture Notes in Computer Science, Springer

We collected a 120-class Indian Sign Language dataset of 9,453 videos and built a model that reaches 89.8% accuracy on it. An entropy-driven SVD sampler drops redundant frames (68.6% fewer), an Elastic Transformer with continuous dual positional encodings handles the temporal side, and a kinematically conditioned adaptive GCN models the hand and body skeleton.

EMBC 2026 Biosignals

Neuromuscular Strategy Extraction from Harmonium Performance via sEMG

Pushp, Anish C. Turlapaty, Purval Bhude, Himangshu Sarma, Mrinmoy Ghorai, Balakrishna Gokaraju

IEEE Engineering in Medicine and Biology Conference (EMBC) 2026

I designed and ran the sEMG data collection with 10 harmonium players, built the synchronised sEMG–audio processing pipeline, and developed a Borda-aggregation feature ranking method combined with PSO-based feature selection to find which muscle features separate players. A 22-feature signature reached 91.8% accuracy, using 75% fewer features than the full set.

A Novel Heuristic-Based Heart Disease Detection Framework for Imbalanced Dataset

Pushp, KG Raghavendra Narayan, Vanga Odelu

IEEE 9th International Conference on Information and Communication Technology (CICT) 2025

A three-stage framework for classifying heart disease severity on the imbalanced Cleveland dataset: class resampling, a tree of four Random Forest classifiers, and a recall-weighted fusion rule that prioritises severe cases. It reached 77.44% accuracy.

Pushp, Vaibhav Prajapati, Himangshu Sarma

Does Personalization Break Trust? Open-Set Rejection Calibration Under Few-Shot On-Device Intent Adaptation

Vaibhav Prajapati, Pushp

Preprint, arXiv submission pending

When an intent classifier is personalised to a user with LoRA on a few examples, its in-scope accuracy goes up, but it gets worse at rejecting out-of-scope inputs. We measure how much open-set rejection calibration degrades under this kind of adaptation.
